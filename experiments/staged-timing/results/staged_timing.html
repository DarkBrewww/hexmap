Staged linear morph

Generated by experiments/staged-timing/staged_analyze.py from the scores of this folder's copy of the morph page. Linear morph only; the Forsch hybrid is unchanged.

The change

In the linear morph every outline point moves straight to its hexagon point, so position, size and shape change together. Here each point is its district's centre plus an offset, and the two run on separate clocks over the same total time. With stagger s, the first clock runs over [0, 1 − s/2] and the second over [s/2, 1]: s = 0 is the linear morph, s = 1 runs one clock in the first half and the other in the second.

  • Outline first: every district turns into its hexagon first and travels afterwards.
  • Travel small: a district larger than its hexagon shrinks first and then travels; a smaller one travels first and grows afterwards. 120 of 436 districts are larger than their hexagon.
  • Three phases (follow-up to the first two): large districts shrink while small ones travel, then the large ones travel, then the small ones grow, so that the large districts travel while the small ones are still small. Phase k = 0, 1, 2 runs over [k s/3, k s/3 + 1 − 2s/3]; at s = 1 each phase has a third of the time.

Checks

  • Stagger 0 reproduces the same run's Linear frames (overlap, self-crossings, area change, covered pixels at all 101 samples): 15 of 15 layout and rule combinations.
  • Those Linear frames match the published scores (evidence/morph_scores.csv and the fixed-grid experiment): 5 of 5 layouts.
  • Largest difference from Linear's outlines at t = 0 and t = 1, all 75 staged runs: 0 screen units.
  • Farthest any staged outline reaches outside the scored rectangle: none (largest value 0.0, negative means inside).
  • Districts larger than their hexagon: 120 of 436 (page and this script agree: yes).
  • All checks passed: True.

Limits

  • Five layouts on one grid, one stagger parameter and 3 rules.
  • Three phases was added after the first two rules were scored, to test an idea their results suggested.
  • Overlap is a raster estimate at 101 samples; pair overlap uses 51 moments.
  • Nothing here measures how easy the animation is to follow. A staged morph has two phases to watch, and faster travel; that needs a user study.
  • The Forsch hybrid is not staged: its precomputed keyframes mix position and outline.
  • District centres are the map centroid and the cell centre; the outline clock uses the same point pairing as the linear morph.
