## How each metric is computed

Positions: region centroids are `center` in the regions file (d3 `geoPath.centroid` of the projected district, screen units, y down). Cells are placed on the unit hex lattice of their orientation from (q, r), so cell vectors are in hex steps (centre-to-centre distance of neighbouring hexes = 1).

- **Location** (McNeill & Hale 2017): least-squares fit of one uniform scale s and a translation t so that s·cell + t best matches the centroids over all regions. Displacement of a region = |s·cell + t − centroid| / (s·step), in hex steps.
- **Adjacency** (Schiewe 2021): map neighbours share a stretch of border (`neighbors`). Pairs kept = map pairs whose cells are hex neighbours. Omission = pairs lost / map pairs. Commission = hex pairs that aren't map pairs ("false pairs") / hex pairs. Isolated tiles = cells with no hex neighbour. Per region: share of its own map neighbours kept, and its number of false hex neighbours.
- **Direction** (Wei et al. 2024): for each map-neighbour pair (a, b), the angle between centroid_b − centroid_a and cell_b − cell_a, 0 to 180 degrees. Over 90 means b is on the wrong side of a.
- **Deform-k** (Meulemans 2026, Section 7; implemented as in GridMapPipeline `analyze/analyzers/Deformation.java`): for region i take its k nearest regions by centroid distance on the map (all other regions for k = all; ties broken by id). With d_j = centroid_j − centroid_i and r_j = cell_j − cell_i (hex steps), Deform-k(i) = min over scalar σ of (1/k) Σ_j |σ d_j − r_j|², solved in closed form (σ = Σ d·r / Σ|d|²). Units are hex steps squared; 0 means the neighbourhood is a scaled copy of the map. Only scale is fitted, no rotation or translation, as in their code and as the paper says ("uniform scaling is not considered distortion"). Two small differences from the paper's wording, both following the code: the paper says "sum of squares", the code (and this script) divides by k, which only rescales each k by a constant; and the paper cites Horn 1987 for the optimal scale, but with only scale to fit the closed form above is the same answer.
- **OrthoOrder-k** (Meulemans 2026; `analyze/analyzers/OrthogonalOrder.java`): same neighbourhood. For each neighbour j, +1 if j is left of i on the map but right of i in the grid (or the reverse), and +1 if j is above i on one but below on the other. A tie on either side (same x, or same y, which is common in a hex grid) never counts (the paper doesn't say how ties are handled; this is the code's rule). Range 0 to 2k, or 2(n − 1) for k = all, as in the paper. k = 5, 15, 50, 100 and all are the paper's own values.
- **Compactness**: hex pairs = pairs of adjacent cells. Roughness (Paul, `hex-layouts/engine.js` `evaluate()`) = (P − P_min) / P_min, where P = 6n − 2·(hex pairs) is the footprint's perimeter in hex sides and P_min is the perimeter of a circle with the same area. Tile components = connected groups of cells.
- **Shape**: the layout's footprint (touching hexes placed by the location fit) against the map's footprint (union of all district polygons). IoU = shared area / combined area, by scanlines 0.25 units apart; 1 means the tile map covers the same ground as the map. Guide-Dev (adapted from Meulemans 2026): for each cell, the fraction of its hexagon that the map footprint doesn't cover; the paper does this per part against the scaled and translated outline of that part, and here the whole map is the outline and the location fit is the scaling and translation, so it works for any generator. Cells fully outside = share of cells with Guide-Dev 100%. Cells outside map = share of fitted cell centres outside the map footprint. The paper's Guide-Miss isn't computed: it flags cells against the pipeline's own guiding shape, which other generators don't have.
- **State structure** (`evidence/layout_scores.py`): a state is split if its cells don't form one connected group; a state border is kept if two states that share a border on the map still have touching cells.
- **Morph**: the morph page's own 101 frames per method (`node evidence/morph_scores.cjs`, the same numbers as its CSV download). Overlap peak and mean over frames, peak self-crossing districts, mean and peak area change. For the linear morph, self-crossings and area change depend only on the drawn hex size, not on which district sits where, so only overlap is in the headline set.

Headline set: the 12 metrics in the table, one or two per facet. Distributions use linear interpolation between ranks for the median and 90th percentile.

## Caveats

- Geometric scores are not proof that a layout is better for readers. Schiewe 2021 ran two surveys with 222 participants each and found the geometric measures showed unclear trends and didn't match what users expected; minimising centroid distance didn't match their choices. Claiming "better for readers" needs a small task-based study.
- Scored on the `mainland` group of the regions file only (3108 regions); neighbour pairs to regions outside it don't count.
