How the right side was made

Schematic outline. __SCHEMATIC__

Directions. Their code knows only rectilinear and octilinear directions. Before the run, x is squashed by tan 30°, which turns the hexagon's slanted sides into 45° sides; after the run it is stretched back. So the allowed directions here are 0°, 30°, 90° and 150°: the hexagon's three edge directions plus horizontal. "On allowed directions" below each side measures the share of the outline's length that runs in one of them (within 1°).

Hexagon inside, then a slide. Their method morphs between outlines that are nested or overlap, while this district's hexagon sits about __SLIDE__ hex steps away on the grid. The hexagon is therefore placed inside the district where it has the most room, their code morphs onto it, and the slide to the grid position is added on top at constant speed. Their code also needs a cut that joins the two outlines; it runs horizontally from the hexagon's upper-left corner to the district's west edge, as the paper asks for a schematic cut line. Untick "Slide to grid position" to see their morph alone.

Center and area. The optional center marker is the polygon's shoelace area centroid, so repeated or deletion vertices do not bias it. In the degenerate zero-area case, the marker uses the outline's bounding-box center. Its dashed line samples the full centroid trajectory. The chart uses the same polygon states and time parameter as the animation.
